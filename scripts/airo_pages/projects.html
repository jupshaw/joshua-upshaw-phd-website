Hidalga Technologies

Translating oncology workflow problems and clinician insights into a responsible AI research and product roadmap.

Full product leadership case study →
OVERVIEW

Founded a healthcare AI venture investigating how clinical evidence readiness, payer-policy requirements, and administrative workflow design can improve prior authorization preparation in oncology.

THE PROBLEM

Oncology practices face fragmented payer rules, missing clinical evidence, disconnected systems, and repeated administrative review. Better decisions require understanding the human workflow, not simply automating forms.

MY ROLE

Founder, CEO, and Principal Investigator. Personally owned the product roadmap, prioritized features, defined requirements, supervised two employees and ten contractors, led NSF-funded R&D, and contributed hands-on research and model development.

APPROACH

Translated discovery findings across more than 340 documented interviews in the broader program into priorities for documentation readiness, evidence extraction, payer-policy retrieval, denial-risk analysis, and human review. Established traceability and uncertainty-handling requirements.

IMPLICATIONS

Connected clinical stakeholder evidence to technical requirements and a prospective validation strategy. These are research and product-development outcomes, not demonstrated production deployment or realized clinic savings.

Selected evidence

  • 340+ documented interviews across the discovery program
  • 12 employees and contractors supervised
  • Human oversight and evidence provenance incorporated into product requirements

Product capabilities include prototypes and development work. No completed live clinical deployment is claimed.

02 / CLINICAL DATA SCIENCE & PREDICTIVE MODELING

Oncology AI Research Pipeline

Reproducible clinical NLP, predictive modeling, longitudinal data analysis, and defensible retrospective evaluation.

Full clinical data science case study →
OVERVIEW

Personally developed a 39-stage Python research pipeline for longitudinal oncology EHR exports, integrating cohort construction, clinical information extraction, classification, model validation, and reproducible reporting.

THE PROBLEM

Retrospective authorization data can be incomplete, imbalanced, and sensitive to time leakage. Extracting clinical evidence and investigating denial risk require careful outcome definitions, patient grouping, and quality controls.

MY ROLE

Hands-on data scientist and primary developer of the retrospective pipeline, feature engineering, evidence extraction, and denial-risk evaluation.

APPROACH

Harmonized heterogeneous clinical records, analyzed missingness and provenance, developed structured and text-based extraction, and evaluated rare-event prediction with patient-grouped splits, PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, and temporal checks.

IMPLICATIONS

Demonstrated feasibility of evidence extraction and retrospective risk modeling while exposing the need for prospective instrumentation and real-workflow validation.

Selected evidence

  • 24,167 oncology patients
  • 200,513 authorization records and 148,703 clinical notes
  • Mean extraction F1: 0.97 across six fields
  • Retrospective denial-risk ROC-AUC: 0.88; PR-AUC: 0.29

All predictive and extraction metrics are from retrospective research. A separate approximately 15% workflow improvement was simulated, not observed operationally.

METHODS & TOOLS

Python · pandas · Parquet · scikit-learn · spaCy · SHAP · statistical validation

03 / COGNITIVE NEUROSCIENCE & EXPERIMENTAL RESEARCH

Smartphone Notifications & Cognitive Control

Experimental investigations of how digital interruption influences behavior and neural measures of attention.

Full neuroscience case study →
OVERVIEW

Designed and conducted human neuroscience experiments examining the behavioral and electrophysiological effects of smartphone notifications during attention-demanding tasks.

THE PROBLEM

Digital notifications compete for attention, but behavioral measurements alone do not explain the neural processes involved or how interventions may affect them.

MY ROLE

First-author researcher responsible for experimental design, participant-study execution, EEG analysis, statistical interpretation, and manuscript preparation.

APPROACH

Used visual oddball paradigms, event-related potentials, spectral EEG, behavioral performance, and a randomized mindfulness/control induction to investigate cognitive control under distraction.

IMPLICATIONS

Produced peer-reviewed evidence relating technological distraction and neural markers of attentional control, with potential applications in human-centered device and interface design.

Selected evidence

  • First-author research in PLOS ONE (2022)
  • First-author research in Biological Psychology (2023)
  • 87 participants in the randomized EEG investigation
  • EEG, ERP, behavioral and mixed-effects analyses

Findings concern experimental mechanisms and measured research outcomes, not clinical treatment effectiveness.

METHODS & TOOLS

BioSemi ActiveTwo · MATLAB · EEGLAB · EEG/ERP · Experimental design · Statistics

04 / EYE TRACKING & EMOTIONAL ATTENTION

Doctoral Dissertation: Emotional Attention & Eye Movements

A preregistered study of emotional facial cues, smartphone notifications, and attentional orientation.

Explore doctoral research →
OVERVIEW

Completed doctoral research examining how emotional faces and smartphone notifications jointly influence visual attention, behavioral performance, and physiological eye-movement measures.

THE PROBLEM

Emotional salience and digital alerts can interact in ways that are not captured by response time alone. Eye tracking allows a more detailed view of overt attentional shifts.

MY ROLE

Doctoral researcher responsible for preregistration, experimental design, implementation, participant sessions, eye-tracking analysis, statistical modeling, and dissertation interpretation.

APPROACH

Used a 360-trial dot-probe task with emotional face stimuli, auditory notifications, and high-frequency eye tracking to study fixations, saccades, pupil responses, and individual differences.

IMPLICATIONS

Connected fine-grained psychophysiological measures with questions of technology-related distraction and affective attention.

Selected evidence

  • Preregistered investigation
  • 50 participants enrolled
  • 360-trial visual attention paradigm
  • Eye movements, pupil measures, and behavioral outcomes

Dissertation findings are original research and should be distinguished from peer-reviewed journal publication.

METHODS & TOOLS

EyeLink eye tracking · Pupillometry · Experimental psychology · R/MATLAB · Statistical inference
