COGNITIVE NEUROSCIENCE · EXPERIMENTAL RESEARCH

How technology competes for attention.

Human neuroscience research using EEG, behavioral experiments, and eye tracking to understand distraction and cognitive control.

The question

What happens to attention and cognitive control when everyday smartphone notifications compete with ongoing cognitive demands? Can brief behavioral interventions modify those effects?

Research design and execution

I designed and conducted human experimental studies using EEG/ERP, spectral analysis, stimulus timing, behavioral performance, and quantitative inference. This work included a randomized study with 87 participants and a preregistered 50-participant eye-tracking investigation.

Selected work

  • Studied electrophysiological and behavioral responses to smartphone notifications during cognitive tasks.
  • Evaluated cognitive control following a brief mindfulness induction using frequency-domain EEG measures.
  • Developed an emotional-attention eye-tracking experiment examining fixation, saccade, pupil, blink, and behavioral measures.
  • Communicated results and limitations through peer-reviewed publications and research presentations.
